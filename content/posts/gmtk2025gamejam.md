After years of thinking about joining a game jam, I finally completed my first game jam entry _The Daily Loop_ for the [GTMK Game Jam 2025](https://itch.io/jam/gmtk-2025). The jam lasted 4 days, starting on July 30th and ending on August 3rd. Given my timezone, the jam would start Wednesday afternoon and end Sunday afternoon. Going into the jam, I chose to use the Unity game engine for the project because my familiarity with it would help me be more productive. I also decided to make a 2D game using pixel art because that would allow me to quickly make art for the game and I did not have experience with creating art in other styles. I expected to spend Wednesday planning the game and knew I would be unable to work on the game Sunday morning, so I would have 3 days to create the game.

When Wednesday afternoon arrived, the theme for the jam was announced: "Loop". I brainstoremed and arrived at the idea of a game that would combine tower defense and city builder elements where the player manages a small town and places buildings to meet the needs of their citizens and earn money. The tower defense rounds would create a gameplay loop, the citizens' needs/routine would create a thematic loop, and I could design the map to have citizens start and end at their house, creating a visual loop. With this plan in place, I began development.

I spent Thursday building a simple map and adding logic to move citizens and place buildings using placeholder sprite shapes provided by Unity. On Friday, I gave citizens procedurally generated needs and added functionality so they could enter buildings that they passed along the track if those buildings addressed one of their needs. I ended Friday by adding a round cycle that would add additional kinds of needs as the game progressed to increase difficulty. I started Saturday by tracking the player's money and adding a screen after the last round that would report the player's stats and whether they met the goal of earning enough money. With the gameplay finished, I created art to replace the placeholder sprites, added sound effects and music, and adjusted aspects like walking speed and sprite scale to improve how the game felt to play. Once I was happy with the game, I made the final build, uploaded it to Itch, and submitted it to the jam.

I'm proud of my first game jam project, and look forward to participating in more in the future.
